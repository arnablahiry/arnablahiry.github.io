Privacy policy

Lararium (Android app) · Last updated 31 October 2026

In short: your family's documents stay on your phone and in your own Google Drive, encrypted. Nothing is sent to the developer, and there is no Lararium server at all. No ads, no analytics, no tracking.

What the app keeps on your phone

  • The documents, photos, details and logins you add, the people, groups and events you create, and your settings.
  • All of it is in an encrypted database (SQLCipher) and encrypted files, with keys held in the phone's secure key storage (Android Keystore), behind your passcode or fingerprint.
  • Text is read from your documents on the phone itself (on-device text recognition); nothing is uploaded to be read.
  • When you crop a profile, group or event picture, the original photo is kept (encrypted) on the phone only, so you can re-frame it later. It is never backed up or shared.

Google sign-in and Google Drive

If you choose “Continue with Google”, the app asks Google for:

  • Your name, email address and profile photo, to show on your account and to know which Drive holds your backup.
  • Access to files the app creates in your Google Drive (drive.file) and to the app's hidden app folder (drive.appdata). The app cannot see any other files in your Drive.

Backups go to a “Lararium” folder in your own Google Drive. Documents and logins are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) before they leave the phone; the backup key is kept in the app's hidden folder in your Drive so you can restore on a new phone. With the optional backup password, your logins are sealed a second time with a key made from a password only you know. You can delete the backup from the app at any time, or revoke the app's access in your Google Account.

Lararium's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to provide the backup, restore and group-sharing features you ask for, is not transferred to anyone else, and is never used for advertising.

Sharing a group with other people

A group is shared between phones with no server in between. Each member's phone puts an encrypted copy of their own and the group's shared documents in their own Drive, shared by link, and reads the other members' copies. A copy can only be opened with the group's code, which travels inside the invite link you send. You decide who gets an invite.

  • The invite link opens a page on this website that hands the invite to the app. The invite is after the “#” in the link, which browsers never send to any server, so this website never receives it.
  • Logins are never shared, except the group's Common logins, which are sealed to each member's phone.
  • Your phone checks the other members' copies regularly (about every minute while the app is open, every 15 minutes in the background) and may show a silent notification of what changed (for example “Family: Jane added Passport”). This happens entirely on your phone.

What the developer receives

Nothing. The developer has no server that receives your documents, details, logins, contacts or usage.

Updates

The app checks GitHub (github.com) for a newer release and its release notes, and, if you agree, downloads the update from there using Android's download manager. GitHub sees an ordinary request from your phone, like any website visit.

Deleting your data

Settings › Delete account removes everything from the phone and, if you choose, the backup from your Drive. You can also revoke Lararium's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
